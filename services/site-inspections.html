The Service

The right inspector for the structure, the defect, and the jurisdiction.

Site and facility inspections are only as useful as the inspector conducting them. A structural engineer evaluating a roofing defect, or a general contractor reviewing a complex MEP failure, produces findings that opposing counsel will dismantle. Criterion matches inspectors to the specific structure type, defect category, and code standards applicable to the matter.

When a case turns on what happened to a building — whether it was built wrong, maintained negligently, or failed to meet code — the inspector who goes on-site needs to be the right person for that specific claim. Criterion evaluates the structure type, the nature of the alleged defect or hazard, the jurisdiction's applicable building codes, and the specific forensic questions the inspection needs to answer before selecting an inspector.

Criterion coordinates site access, schedules inspection dates, manages communication with opposing counsel where joint inspections are required, and tracks inspection report delivery. We handle the logistics so the attorney can focus on the case.

"An inspection report from a specialist whose expertise precisely matches the defect category carries far more evidentiary weight than one from a generalist who happens to be licensed in the right state."

How It Works

From case submission to inspection report

Criterion manages every coordination step. You provide the case details and site access information — we handle the rest.

1
Submit your case and site details
Provide a description of the structure, the alleged defect or hazardous condition, the jurisdiction, site access status, and any scheduling constraints. Note whether the inspection is ex parte or whether joint inspection with opposing counsel is required or anticipated.
2
Inspector matching
Criterion identifies inspectors whose expertise directly matches the structure type, defect category, and applicable code standards. For construction defect, we evaluate experience with the specific construction method, materials, and code era relevant to the matter. For premises liability, we match to the specific hazard type and occupancy category.
3
Site access coordination
We coordinate with the property owner, opposing counsel, or managing agent to confirm site access dates. Where joint inspection protocols apply, Criterion manages the scheduling communication and confirms logistical arrangements with all parties.
4
On-site inspection and documentation
The inspector conducts the on-site evaluation, documents conditions through photographs and field notes, and collects any material samples or measurements required for the forensic analysis. Criterion confirms inspection completion and initiates report preparation.
5
Written report and documentation delivered
You receive the inspection report with findings, photographic documentation, and the inspector's forensic conclusions. Criterion tracks the report timeline and follows up with the inspector if delivery is delayed beyond the agreed turnaround window.

When to Use This Service

Cases that require an on-site evaluation

  • Construction defect claims — residential, multi-family, or commercial — where the physical condition of the structure needs independent forensic documentation before mediation or trial.
  • Premises liability cases where the hazardous condition needs to be documented before it is remediated, altered, or lost to natural deterioration.
  • Time-sensitive matters where site conditions are at risk of change — construction being completed, repairs being made, or evidence being removed — and rapid inspection is critical.
  • Joint defense inspections where the defense needs its own qualified inspector present alongside plaintiff's expert to observe and document the same conditions simultaneously.
  • HOA and condominium disputes where the alleged construction defects span multiple units or common area components requiring systematic forensic survey.
  • Workplace injury cases where the condition of the worksite, equipment, or facility is central to the liability theory.
  • Environmental matters where on-site sampling, air quality testing, or contamination mapping requires a qualified specialist with both technical expertise and litigation experience.
