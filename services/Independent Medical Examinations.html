The Service

More than finding a physician. Managing the entire process.

An independent medical examination requires a physician whose specialty directly matches the injury or condition at issue — and a coordination process that keeps the examination on schedule, properly documented, and within the procedural requirements of the jurisdiction.

Criterion handles every element of IME coordination. We identify board-certified physicians matched to the specific injury type and case context, manage scheduling and examinee communication logistics, track report turnaround, and deliver the final report. You submit the case — we manage the process from physician selection through report delivery.

Physician matching is the most consequential step. An orthopedic surgeon reviewing a traumatic brain injury claim, or a general practitioner evaluating a complex spinal injury, creates unnecessary vulnerability. We match examiners to the precise condition at issue — specialty, sub-specialty, and where relevant, the specific treatment modality or surgical intervention being disputed.

"The right IME physician isn't just board-certified in the right specialty — they're currently practicing, familiar with the applicable standard of care, and able to produce a report that holds up to cross-examination."

Criterion coordinates IMEs across all major injury types and practice areas, including personal injury, workers' compensation defense, medical malpractice, and long-term disability claims. Geographic coverage is nationwide, with examiners available in all major jurisdictions.

How It Works

From case submission to final report

Criterion manages the complete IME process. Here is how each engagement runs.

1
Submit your case request
Provide the injury type, relevant medical history summary, jurisdiction, examinee name and contact information, and any scheduling constraints or deadlines. Note whether the examination is for defense, independent evaluation, or future care assessment.
2
Physician selection
Criterion identifies board-certified physicians matched to the specific injury or condition, with current active practice status in the relevant specialty. We confirm availability and geographic fit before presenting the physician for your approval.
3
Scheduling and logistics
We manage all scheduling communication directly with the examining physician's office. Examinee notice letters, appointment confirmation, and any pre-examination records transmission are coordinated by Criterion. You receive confirmation of the appointment date, time, location, and examining physician.
4
Examination and report tracking
After the examination, Criterion tracks report turnaround against the agreed timeline and follows up with the examining physician's office as needed. Delays are flagged proactively — you are not left waiting and wondering.
5
Report delivery
The completed IME report is transmitted to you directly. If any issues arise with the report — incomplete examination notes, missing sections, or discrepancies — Criterion coordinates resolution with the physician before delivery.

When to Use This Service

Cases that call for an IME

  • Personal injury defense where plaintiff's claimed injuries exceed what the accident mechanism can plausibly explain.
  • Workers' compensation defense where the extent of disability, work restrictions, or need for ongoing treatment is disputed.
  • Long-term disability claims where the insurer requires independent evaluation of a claimant's condition and functional capacity.
  • Medical malpractice defense where the current state of the plaintiff's condition needs independent documentation separate from the treating physician's records.
  • Cases involving claims of future medical treatment needs or permanent disability — where the defense needs an independent prognosis.
  • Situations where the treating physician's reports are inconsistent, incomplete, or appear to advocate for the patient rather than document objectively.
  • High-value claims where independent medical documentation is essential before settlement negotiations or trial.
