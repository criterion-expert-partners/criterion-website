Peer Review & Record Analysis

Independent clinical evaluation of medical records and case files by a specialist matched to the standard of care and case requirements. Written opinion—no testimony required.

The Service

What Peer Review Includes

A peer review evaluation begins with our assessment of your case and the clinical or technical questions at hand. We then identify and engage a specialist whose credentials and experience match the specific standard of care or practice area in issue. The reviewer evaluates all materials you provide—medical records, prior expert reports, discovery, depositions—and produces a detailed written opinion addressing the medical merit of the plaintiff's claims or the defendant's position.

This is a consulting engagement. No testimony or public disclosure is required unless you later decide to retain the reviewer as a testifying expert.

Common Use Cases

When Peer Review Makes Sense

Early Case Assessment — Before committing to a full expert engagement, a peer review opinion tells you whether the case has merit from a clinical standpoint and where the vulnerabilities lie.

Evaluating Opposing Experts — In defense matters, a peer review challenges the methodology and conclusions of plaintiff experts and can identify gaps or departures from the standard of care in their opinions.

Settlement Support — An independent clinical opinion strengthens your settlement position by documenting the strengths and weaknesses of the opposing side's medical claims.

Post-Deposition Analysis — After deposing a plaintiff's expert, a peer review opinion can expose inconsistencies or departures from accepted practice standards that were revealed during questioning.
